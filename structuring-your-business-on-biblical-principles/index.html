Structuring Your Business on Biblical Principles

Jacob Gonzales November 10, 2026

Entering into a business partnership means choosing to navigate a long, often challenging journey alongside someone else. For Christian entrepreneurs, a business is an extension of faith. One that should be built on a foundation of integrity, stewardship, and unity. However, unity does not happen by accident. In the business world, biblical agreement is practically applied through a well-drafted Operating Agreement. This same operating agreement applies for all business owners, whether you are a partnership or sole entity. You should have a physical document that reminds you how you are to conduct your business in a Christ honoring manner that directly reflects the teachings of the bible. 

An Operating Agreement is more than just a legal formality to file away; it is the living blueprint of your company. It establishes clear expectations for governance, conflict resolution, and the stewardship of the resources God has entrusted to you. For businesses with two or more owners, this document serves as a mutual agreement, guiding how the company will operate day-to-day and year-to-year. It is recommended that this document is updated and frequently read as your business grows.

Below is a practical breakdown of what your Operating Agreement should include to ensure your business structure reflects both professional excellence and biblical wisdom. Ensure to read into your states local laws to ensure compliance within the state that you conduct business in.

Initial Contributions

Before a business can grow, it requires a seed. Your agreement must explicitly detail what each partner is bringing to the table at the very beginning.

  • Capital Contributions: Exactly how much cash is each member investing?
  • Sweat Equity & Assets: Is one partner contributing property, equipment, or specialized labor instead of cash?

Writing this down prevents future resentment and ensures that every member's initial sacrifice is recognized and recorded.

Ownership Percentages & Division of Shares

Proverbs 11:1 reminds us that "A false balance is abomination to the Lord: but a just weight is his delight." Your agreement must clearly define how the pie is divided.

  • Equity Split: What percentage of the company does each member own?

  • Profit and Loss Allocation: Does the distribution of profits and losses match the ownership percentages, or is there a different structural arrangement?

Clarity here prevents the root of bitterness from taking hold when the business becomes profitable.

Roles, Responsibilities, and the Registered Agent

A business without defined leadership is a ship without a rudder. The agreement needs to outline exactly who is responsible for what.

  • Day-to-Day Operations: Who has the authority to sign contracts, hire employees, or make financial decisions?

  • The Registered Agent: You must designate a specific person (or service) responsible for receiving official legal and state mail. This ensures you never miss critical compliance notices or legal actions.

  • IRS and Tax Representation: Who is the designated "Tax Matters Partner" or representative for the IRS? This section also dictates how the business is professionally structured for tax purposes (e.g., as a partnership, S-Corp election, etc.).

Meetings and Voting Rights

Proverbs 15:22 reminds us that "Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established." Your operating agreement should act as your framework for taking counsel together. If you are unable to take counsel together or resolve issues internally, you need to have a trusted advisor who owns a fractional share of the company. You should be conducting business in a matter that upholds the same character that you present in the church.

  • Shareholder/Member Meetings: How often will you meet formally? Annually? Quarterly?

  • Voting Rights: Do all members have equal voting power, or is it proportional to their ownership percentage?

  • Major Decisions: Define what requires a simple majority vote (51%) versus a unanimous vote (100%) such as taking on major debt, bringing in a new partner, or selling company assets.

Dissolution, Exits, and Sales

Ecclesiastes teaches us that there is a season for everything. There may come a day when a partner wants to leave, or the entire business needs to be sold or dissolved. Failing to plan for an exit is one of the most common ways partnerships end in strife or even lawsuits.

  • Buy-Sell Agreements: If one partner wants out, or tragically passes away, how is their share valued? Does the remaining partner have the right of first refusal to buy those shares?

  • Sale of the Business: What is the protocol if an outside buyer offers to purchase the company?

  • Dissolution: If the business must close, how are the remaining assets liquidated, debts paid, and the final balances distributed?

Execution and the Wet Signature

A "wet signature" a physical, ink-on-paper signature acts as this formal seal. It elevates the agreement from a casual understanding to a tangible, binding commitment.

  • Binding Commitment: Have all members physically signed the document? In most states, signatures from all members are required to make the agreement legally binding.   
  • Dating the Document: What is the official effective date of the agreement? (This can be the date of formation or the date of signing).   
  • Notarization: Does the agreement include provisions, such as real estate transfers, that would legally require the signatures to be notarized?   
  • Record Keeping: Has every member received a signed copy, and is the original, wet-signed document safely stored in your business records?   

Drafting a comprehensive Operating Agreement is an act of wisdom and love for your business partners. By explicitly defining the structure, shares, operations, and exit strategies upfront, you are honoring the principle of Amos 3:3 "Can two walk together, except they be agreed?". You are ensuring that before you take that first step together, you are truly in agreement equipped to steward your business for the glory of God and HIS kingdom.
